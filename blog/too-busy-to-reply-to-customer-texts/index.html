Too busy to reply to customer texts? A simple system that stops you losing jobs

2026-11-09 · 5 min read

It is a common problem for trades and service businesses: marketing starts working, calls and texts increase, and you are too busy doing the work to answer them. Meanwhile, the customer who texted at 10am has already messaged someone else by lunchtime.

Step 1: acknowledge fast, even if you cannot answer fully

A reply within a minute that says "Thanks, we got your message and will be in touch shortly" keeps the customer waiting for you instead of shopping around. This is the easiest piece to automate.

Step 2: collect the basics up front

Let the first automatic message ask a few fixed questions, so you are not starting from zero when you do reply:

  • What do you need help with?
  • What is the address or area?
  • How urgent is it?
  • Can you send a photo? (where it helps)

Step 3: sort by urgency

Emergencies, quotes and general questions need different handling. Tag each enquiry so the urgent ones reach your phone and the rest wait in a clean list for when you are free.

Step 4: hand over to a human at the right moment

Automation should handle the first reply and the routine questions. Pricing talks, sensitive situations and anything unusual are for you or your team. Make it easy to take over, and tell people they are chatting with the business, not pretend to be a person.

Step 5: keep the rules and consent straight

  • Only text people who contacted you or agreed to hear from you.
  • Always allow people to opt out.
  • Check the texting rules in your country before you start.
